## 0. How Codex Should Use This Document

This document is the current product and UX foundation for the Pathwisse public website.

Before making product, UX, content, design, or implementation decisions:

1. Read the repository root `AGENTS.md`.
2. Read any applicable Codex-specific guidance under `.codex/`.
3. Follow the installed ECC workflow and relevant skills.
4. Read this document completely.
5. Treat this document as the product/UX source of truth until superseded by the final PRD.
6. Do not invent product capabilities, proof points, testimonials, metrics, partnerships, outcomes, or customer claims.
7. Do not begin major implementation work if it would require assumptions that materially change the website strategy documented here.

10. Any future PRD should refine this blueprint rather than silently contradict it.

### Current build philosophy

The Pathwisse website must not become a collection of generic SaaS cards, gradients, buzzwords, and disconnected feature blocks.

The website should feel like a coherent story.

The intended visitor journey is:

**Recognition → Tension → New perspective → Solution → Understanding → Trust → Personal relevance → Action**

In Pathwisse terms:

**“This is my problem” → “The current journey is fragmented” → “Employability needs to be connected” → “This is Pathwisse” → “I understand how it works” → “I believe it is credible” → “This is relevant to me” → “I know what to do next.”**

# 1. Product Context

## 1.1 Core statement

> **We’re rebuilding the last mile of education: getting hired.**

Pathwisse is the **Employability Operating System for higher education**: the layer between:

> **“I learned this.”**  
> and  
> **“I can prove it.”**

Built by **Shaquantum Labs Private Limited**, Pathwisse connects career choice, structured learning, applied work, evidence of capability, and placement preparation.

---

## 1.2 Why Pathwisse exists

Learning and getting hired are often treated as separate journeys.

Students collect courses, certificates, workshops, and projects, yet can still struggle to explain what they can actually do.

Colleges and universities often need visibility into student readiness before placement season, while there is still time to guide and intervene.

Pathwisse exists to connect those fragmented stages into a coherent employability journey.

# 3. Audience Priority

The website serves multiple audiences, but they are not equally important.

## Priority 1 — Students

Primary emotional and conversion audience.

The student should quickly understand:

- why Pathwisse is relevant,
- what problem it solves,
- how their learning connects to employability,
- what they gain,
- what they should do next.

### Primary student action

**Get started / Start your journey / Sign up**

Exact CTA vocabulary will be finalized in the content PRD.

---

## Priority 2 — Colleges and Universities

Primary institutional buying / lead-generation audience.

The institution should understand:

- why employability readiness should be visible before placement season,
- how Pathwisse fits between education and employment,
- what visibility or structure Pathwisse can provide,
- why the platform deserves a conversation.

### Primary institution action

**Request a Demo**

---

## Priority 3 — Placement Teams / TPOs

Placement teams and Training & Placement Officers are a critical institutional stakeholder.

Their needs should be addressed primarily within the institution journey rather than fragmenting the site into too many audience pages at the beginning.

# 7. Differentiation Status

A fully validated competitive differentiator has **not yet been established**.

Do not convert an unvalidated hypothesis into a factual marketing claim.

## Working differentiation hypothesis

> Most learning platforms focus on helping students consume or complete learning. Pathwisse is designed to connect learning with application, evidence of capability, and hiring readiness.

This should be investigated further before being framed as an external comparative claim.

## Important strategic concept: Evidence

The idea of **evidence** may become central to the Pathwisse brand.

Instead of asking only:

> “Did you complete the course?”

Pathwisse wants the journey to move toward:

> “What can you demonstrate?”

This creates a stronger relationship between learning and employability.

# 8. Credibility Constraint

At the current stage, there are no approved quantitative proof points for the public website.

Do **not** fabricate:

- student counts,
- partner counts,
- university logos,
- placement rates,
- salary increases,
- course completion rates,
- hiring partner lists,
- NPS,
- awards,
- testimonials,
- case studies,
- employment statistics.

Until real proof exists, credibility should be created using:

1. clarity,
2. methodology,
3. product depth,
4. transparent explanations,
5. thoughtful product visuals,
6. well-defined student and institution problems,
7. company information,
8. privacy / data transparency,
9. authentic examples clearly marked as illustrative.

Future proof components should be designed so verified metrics, testimonials, university relationships, and case studies can be added later without restructuring the entire site.

# 17. Content Principles

## 17.1 Clarity before cleverness

A headline should help a visitor understand the product, not merely sound impressive.

## 17.2 Human language

Prefer:

> “What should I learn next?”

over:

> “Navigate personalized competency acquisition pathways.”

## 17.3 Specificity

Prefer concrete outcomes, actions, and problems over abstract brand language.

## 17.4 No unsupported claims

Never transform assumptions into marketing facts.

## 17.5 Progressive disclosure

Homepage = narrative.

Secondary pages = detail.

Do not force every product concept onto the homepage.

## 17.6 Audience-aware language

Student copy should feel personal and understandable.

Institution copy should feel credible, operational, and outcome-oriented.

Neither should sound bureaucratic.

# 18. Psychological Principles Behind the Flow

The final PRD will expand this section.

## 18.1 Recognition before persuasion

People pay attention when they see their own problem reflected accurately.

Therefore the homepage first surfaces the student's fragmented experience before introducing Pathwisse.

## 18.2 Problem before product

Introducing the product too early forces the visitor to understand a solution before they understand why it exists.

The website should establish the employability gap first.

## 18.3 Cognitive load reduction

Fewer choices improve comprehension.

This is why the navigation, CTA strategy, page count, and homepage components should remain intentional and limited.

## 18.4 Concrete over abstract

A student story or visible journey is easier to understand than a wall of capability labels.

## 18.5 Trust through honesty

Because Pathwisse currently lacks approved public proof metrics, honesty is strategically stronger than fabricated scale.

## 18.6 Progressive commitment

The visitor should not be pushed immediately into a high-friction action.

The flow should allow:

**Understand → Explore → Believe → Act**

## 18.7 Identity and aspiration

Students are not merely purchasing courses.

The website should help them imagine becoming someone who can credibly demonstrate capability in a hiring context.

## 18.8 Institutional urgency without fear

Institution copy should make the timing problem clear:

**If readiness becomes visible only during placement season, intervention may already be late.**

This should not become manipulative fear-based copy.

# 19. Visual and Motion Principles

Motion should explain.

It should not exist simply because modern websites use motion.

## Preferred uses

- showing progression,
- connecting one stage to another,
- demonstrating cause and effect,
- transitioning between learning and evidence,
- showing a student's journey,
- revealing product context,
- reinforcing hierarchy,
- guiding attention.

## Avoid

- constant floating UI cards,
- excessive parallax,
- motion on every element,
- generic particle backgrounds,
- decorative gradients as the main identity,
- autoplay visual noise,
- interactions that delay reading,
- 3D scenes unrelated to the story,
- animation that hurts accessibility or performance.

## Visual direction

Premium technology + human/student storytelling.

Potential media can include:

- cinematic student moments,
- motion graphics,
- clean product UI,
- editorial illustrations,
- restrained diagrams,
- authentic educational environments.

Visuals should support the narrative rather than decorate empty space.

# 23. Product Claims Register

During PRD development, every important public claim should be classified as one of:

### VERIFIED

Supported by real product/company evidence.

### APPROVED POSITIONING

Brand framing approved for public communication, but not presented as a measurable proof claim.

### HYPOTHESIS

A strategic thought requiring validation.

### ILLUSTRATIVE

A fictional example used only to explain a journey.

### PROHIBITED UNTIL VERIFIED

Metrics, testimonials, results, partnerships, rankings, comparative superiority, or outcome claims without evidence.

Codex should preserve these distinctions.

# 26. Codex Guardrails for the Pathwisse Build

Codex must not treat this project as a prompt to “make a beautiful landing page.”

Before implementing a major page or feature, validate that it contributes to the documented story.

## Do

- read relevant ECC / project instructions,
- research when uncertainty is material,
- reuse components,
- maintain design consistency,
- keep layouts intentional,
- build accessible responsive interfaces,
- use meaningful motion,
- maintain clean content hierarchy,
- document important decisions,
- test implementation,
- preserve user-approved content,
- flag unverified claims.

## Do not

- invent business information,
- rewrite the strategy silently,
- add sections simply to fill space,
- use placeholder statistics as if real,
- create fictional testimonials,
- overload navigation,
- create generic SaaS feature grids by default,
- overuse glassmorphism,
- add uncontrolled animation,
- introduce an unnecessary framework or dependency,
- optimize for visual novelty over comprehension.
