# Pathwisse public website: UX psychology

**Status:** Working UX rationale; pre-PRD  
**Depends on:** `PATHWISSE_WEBSITE_BLUEPRINT.md`, `POSITIONING.md`, `VOCABULARY.md`, `MESSAGING.md`

This document defines the psychological logic of the public website. It is not production copy and does not authorize invented capabilities, proof, metrics, testimonials, or outcomes. Audience assumptions remain research hypotheses.

## 1. Psychological north star

The intended journey is **Recognition → Tension → New perspective → Solution → Understanding → Trust → Personal relevance → Action**.

In Pathwisse terms: “This feels familiar” → “This problem is bigger than I thought” → “There is another way to think about employability” → “This is what Pathwisse is” → “Now I understand how it works” → “This seems credible” → “This is relevant to me” → “I know what to do next.”

Recognition earns attention through familiarity. Tension names fragmentation without blame. New perspective reframes employability as a connected journey. Solution introduces Pathwisse only after the need is understood. Understanding provides a manageable model. Trust comes from clarity, boundaries, and available evidence. Personal relevance translates the model into value. Action then feels reasonable rather than pressured.

This is better than leading with features because feature-first communication asks visitors to interpret an unfamiliar system before they understand why it exists.

## 3. Audience psychology

These are hypotheses requiring research, not universal truths.

### Students

- **Motivations:** direction, relevant work, clearer capability, and confidence in opportunity conversations.
- **Anxieties:** choosing poorly, wasting time, placement pressure, and certificates that do not explain capability.
- **Objections:** “Is this another course platform?”, “Will it help me get hired?”, “Is it only for final-year students?”, and “Will it take too much time?”
- **Questions:** What is it? What should I work on next? What counts as evidence? Is it for my stage?
- **Trust triggers:** plain language, visible relevance, approved product detail, honest limits, and transparent data practice.
- **Trust breakers:** generic EdTech claims, pressure, fake outcomes, unexplained AI, and dashboard-like feature walls.
- **Desired progression:** recognition → relief from self-blame → direction → practical confidence → reasonable action.

Research: how students define employability, what certificates and projects prove to them, when placement concern begins, and what makes them trust or abandon a platform.

### Colleges and universities

- **Motivations:** visibility into development, coordinated support, applied work, and earlier employability conversations.
- **Concerns:** adoption effort, alignment, participation, privacy, credibility, and operational complexity.
- **Objections:** “How is this different from our LMS or placement software?”, “Who uses it?”, “What data is collected?”, and “What proof exists?”
- **Trust triggers:** company identity, methodology, scope, privacy information, implementation clarity, and honest validation status.
- **Trust breakers:** unsupported outcomes, invented institutional results, vague AI, opaque data, and ignoring faculty work.
- **Desired progression:** relevance → operational clarity → lower perceived risk → context-specific evaluation.

Research: current tools, intervention timing, buying roles, internal terminology, data requirements, and decision criteria.

### Placement teams / TPOs

- **Motivations:** earlier visibility, coordinated training and mentorship, and informed support conversations.
- **Concerns:** added workload, engagement, unreliable signals, and pressure to deliver outcomes the product cannot control.
- **Objections:** “Will this add a process?”, “How is readiness measured?”, and “What evidence exists?”
- **Trust triggers:** specific workflow, permissions, privacy boundaries, real product demonstration, and respect for existing teams.
- **Trust breakers:** claiming replacement of people, universal readiness scores, or placement guarantees.
- **Desired progression:** visibility problem → support model → implementation questions → relevant conversation.

## 5. Hero, recognition, and tension

The hero should answer quickly: what is this, is it relevant, and why continue. Target comprehension: **five seconds** identifies the learning/employability context; **ten seconds** understands structured direction, demonstrable capability, and preparation; **thirty seconds** understands the connected journey and its limits. The hero should not explain every feature, audience, program, company detail, or methodology.

“Employability Operating System” provides category distinctiveness but is abstract. Pair it immediately with plain functional language such as connecting learning with work a student can show. Category language should not require visitors to learn internal terminology before they understand value.

Courses, certificates, workshops, projects, and interviews work as recognition points because they are familiar. Together they reveal tension without implying that the student did something wrong. The governing tone is: **“You have done the work. The journey is simply disconnected.”** Avoid “You will be unemployable,” “Your degree is worthless,” “AI will take your job,” “You are falling behind,” “Your college is failing students,” or “Your outcomes will collapse.” Legitimate urgency comes from fragmentation and timing.

## 6. How-it-works, story, proof, and value

Four stages chunk the model and create progressive understanding: **Find your direction**, **Build intentionally**, **Turn learning into evidence**, **Prepare to prove it**. Each stage answers one question and prepares the next; it is not a universal score or guarantee.

An illustrative student such as Aanya reduces abstraction and lets visitors imagine continuity. It must be labeled **Illustrative student journey**, never presented as a testimonial.

Product UI should appear after the problem and model. It is evidence that a real product exists, not decoration. Use the fewest approved screens that establish a coherent workflow; too many create comparison overload. Never use fake dashboards, unverified workflows, or illustrative data presented as real.

Translate feature → benefit → personal meaning:

| If verified          | Benefit                                   | Personal meaning                                     |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Role mapping         | See skills associated with a target role. | Know what to work on next.                           |
| Structured path      | Follow an ordered development sequence.   | Spend effort with more direction.                    |
| Applied project      | Practice in a concrete context.           | Have work to discuss.                                |
| Evidence record      | Organize context around work.             | Explain contribution, not just activity.             |
| Preparation activity | Rehearse communication.                   | Enter an opportunity conversation with more clarity. |

## 7. Institution transition and programs

The institution transition should feel like the same problem at another level: **if students need a connected employability journey, institutions need visibility into that journey.** The homepage is student-first because students are the primary emotional and conversion audience; institutions appear later once the shared model is understood. The institutional story should address credibility, relevance, operational clarity, proof, risk reduction, and implementation confidence. Transparency and methodology help but do not replace outcome evidence or references.

Programs appear after the system is understood. Otherwise Pathwisse can look like a course marketplace. The intended order is **system → journey → program as one intervention**, not **catalogue → brand**. Explain program purpose before modules, pricing, delivery, or outcomes; all exact details require validation.

## 9. CTA, navigation, motion, and visual psychology

Student progression is **Learn → Explore → Understand → Get Started**. Institution progression is **Understand → Evaluate → Build confidence → Request Demo**. Low-friction CTAs include “See how it works”; medium-friction actions include audience or program exploration; high-friction actions include signup and contact forms. Use CTA zones in the hero, audience transition, program decision point, and final section. Avoid a CTA after every section.

Small navigation reduces choice overload and improves information scent. The working architecture is **How It Works, For Students, For Institutions, Programs, About**, with Sign In, Get Started, and Request Demo clearly differentiated by destination and audience.

Motion may direct attention, explain sequencing, connect concepts, or demonstrate progression. Use slow scroll reveals, restrained path drawing, and subtle transitions only when they explain. Avoid continuous loops, forced waiting, sensory overload, background motion behind reading, or animation that hides information. A static state must retain the same meaning; reduced-motion preferences must be honored.

Visuals should do emotional or explanatory work, ideally both. Cinematic educational environments can create recognition; journey diagrams can explain progression; approved UI can establish functional trust. Avoid stock-photo overload, fake dashboards, unreadable diagrams, and outcome-signaling imagery that cannot be supported.

Mobile is not compressed desktop: preserve narrative order, reduce density, keep the primary CTA findable, make essential content understandable without motion, and avoid parallax that causes discomfort.

## 10. Forms, density, and objections

Student signup and institution demo forms should request only information necessary to begin the stated conversation. Exact fields require validation. Explain why information is collected, place privacy reassurance near the form, use progressive disclosure, and provide specific recoverable errors. Avoid preselected consent and ambiguous submit labels.

Ethical persuasion excludes false urgency, scarcity, social proof, fear, countdowns without real deadlines, manipulative opt-ins, dark patterns, preselected consent, and misleading CTA labels.

## 12. Decision principles

1. If a section does not answer a meaningful visitor question, remove it.
2. If the next section does not answer the current section’s question, reorder or rewrite.
3. If a claim cannot be demonstrated, classify it as a hypothesis or omit it.
4. Explain benefits before internal terminology.
5. If a visual is beautiful but not clarifying, connecting, or contextualizing, simplify it.
6. If animation cannot explain why it exists, remove it; the static state must remain complete.
7. Explain the value of a high-friction form before requesting information.
8. Connect each new audience to the same employability problem before changing tone.
9. Label fictional stories clearly.
10. Increase transparency when evidence is missing; do not increase certainty language.
11. If mobile needs desktop motion to make sense, the content model is unfinished.
12. Do not publish unverified metrics, outcomes, partnerships, testimonials, capabilities, or guarantees.

## 13. Open research questions

### Students

- How do students describe employability and readiness?
- What do they think certificates and projects prove?
- When does placement concern begin?
- What makes a career platform credible or easy to abandon?
- Which next step feels useful rather than burdensome?

### Institutions

- What readiness data do TPOs and faculty actually need?
- When do institutions intervene and what enables intervention?
- Which tools and processes already exist?
- Who influences purchase, implementation, and renewal?
- What privacy, permission, and governance requirements are non-negotiable?

### Product and business

- Which capabilities are live and publicly demonstrable?
- What exactly does “evidence” mean, and what can it not prove?
- Is “Employability Operating System” understood by target audiences?
- What does “Start Your Journey” lead to?
- What are the access, pricing, program, and implementation models?
- Which trust signals can be approved for public use?

These are research needs, not blockers for this document. Resolve them before stronger claims or final production copy.
